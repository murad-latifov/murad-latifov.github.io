Research

Job Market Paper

The Impact of Professional Sports Franchise Movements on Urban Crime Rates

Municipalities often justify large public subsidies for professional sports franchises through expected social benefits, yet the long-term externalities on public safety remain underexplored. I find that these crime externalities are highly sensitive to market saturation. In solo markets, a city's first franchise structurally increases overall crime, while the departure of a sole franchise produces a proportional decrease. In established multi-team markets, adding or losing a franchise yields no statistically significant change.
